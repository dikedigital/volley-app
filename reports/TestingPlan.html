Funnel Meta Ads Testing Plan
Testing Plan

Four phases. One gate each.

Four conversion gates that matter:

  1. Page to call start
  2. Call start to call completion
  3. Results screen to account creation
  4. Account creation to paid (day 30)

Each phase tests one gate. No phase starts until the previous one is locked.

4 conversion gates · NPI baseline — cold pixel · Kill date: day 21 for Phase 1
NPI baseline note

No historical data. Cold pixel. No existing audience. This means:

Early CPMs will run high — Meta's algorithm needs 50+ conversion events per ad set before it exits the learning phase. Budget for this. Do not kill angles in the first 7 days.

Absolute cost benchmarks do not exist yet. All kill decisions in Phase 1 are relative — angle vs. angle — not against an external standard.

Phases

1

Which ad angle works

Gate tested: Ad → Call start
Duration: Sample-driven. Minimum 100 call starts per angle before any kill decision.

Three ad sets. One angle each. Equal daily budget.

All traffic to the landing page. Do not change the page during Phase 1.

Angle 4 held back as retargeting only until Phases 1–2 are complete.

Setup
Day 1–7 Optimize for landing page views while Meta exits the learning phase.
Day 7+ Switch to call starts once each ad set has 50+ events.
Day 21 Kill date. Set the calendar block now and do not review before it.
Min 100 call starts per angle before any kill decision.
Metrics
Metric Role
Call start rate (page visits → call starts) Primary — did this angle attract the right person?
Call completion rate (started → finished) Quality check — did they stay through 15 minutes?
Cost per call completion Efficiency — what did it cost to get a complete diagnosis?
30-day trial-to-paid rate by angle Buyer quality — the modifier that overrides cost
Kill logic

Cost per call completion is the kill trigger. Trial-to-paid is the override.

If Angle B costs 2× Angle A per completion with a similar trial-to-paid rate: kill Angle B, move budget to Angle A.

If Angle B costs 2× Angle A but trial-to-paid is meaningfully stronger: calculate cost per paid subscriber for each. The cheaper completion is not always the winner.

If call completion rate is low across all three simultaneously: the problem is not the angle. It is the call experience. Do not kill angles — fix the call first, then restart.

2

Call framing

Gate tested: Page → Call start
Traffic: Winning angle from Phase 1 only
Duration: Minimum 200 call starts across both variants

The page CTA is "Start your first conversation." This phase tests whether the framing of what happens after the click affects call start rate and — more importantly — call completion rate.

Same call. Different expectation the visitor arrives with. Expectation shapes perceived value of the results.

What you're testing
Control

Current CTA framing: "Start your first conversation. Free. 15 minutes. No account."

Variant

Diagnosis framing: "Find out exactly where your [language] breaks down. Free. 15 minutes."

Metrics
Metric What it tells you
Call start rate Did one framing earn more clicks into the call?
Call completion rate Did one framing attract people more likely to finish?
Results screen engagement (scroll, time) Did one framing create higher motivation to read the output?
Account creation rate Did one framing produce more conversions at the offer moment?
Kill logic

If completion rate diverges by more than 15% between variants after 200 call starts: the framing is doing real work. Lock the winner.

If completion rates are similar but account creation rate differs: the framing affects how people receive the results, not just whether they start. Lock the winner based on account creation rate.

3

Results screen and offer

Gate tested: Results screen → Account creation
Traffic: Winning angle + winning call framing
Duration: Minimum 200 completed calls across both variants

The results screen is the highest-leverage moment in the funnel. The user has just experienced the product. They have a personalised assessment in front of them. This is where $99/month first appears. Small changes here have outsized downstream impact.

What you're testing
Control

Assessment, 6-week plan, and offer presented in sequence — full detail on all three sections visible on load.

Variant

Assessment only visible on load. Plan and offer revealed progressively as the user scrolls or taps "see your plan." A user who actively reveals the plan has expressed more intent.

Metrics
Metric What it tells you
Account creation rate Primary — did the screen convert?
Time on results screen Did people read it or bounce?
30-day trial-to-paid rate Did the screen attract committed buyers or curious browsers?
60-day retention Did one variant produce users who stayed longer?
Kill logic

Account creation rate is the primary kill signal. If one variant produces 10%+ higher account creation after 200 completed calls, lock it regardless of time-on-screen.

If account creation rates are similar but 30-day trial-to-paid diverges: lock the variant with higher trial-to-paid. A user who converted to paid is worth more than two who created accounts and churned.

4

Scaling and Angle 4

Unlocked after: Phases 1–3 complete, winning combination locked

At this point you have a baseline cost per paid subscriber. Angle 4's performance is measured against that number, not against Phase 1 benchmarks.

Deploy Angle 4 as
1 Retargeting against all page visitors who did not start the call.
2 Cold traffic test against the winning angle from Phase 1. Measured against baseline cost per paid subscriber.
Triage

What to kill first if numbers come in soft

Soft numbers across all three phases mean one of two things. Work through them in order before changing anything else.

1

Check call completion rate first. If fewer than 70% of users who start the call finish it, the call itself is the problem. No amount of ad or page optimization fixes a leaky call experience. Pause paid spend, fix the call, restart Phase 1.

2

Check the results screen drop-off. If call completion is strong but account creation is weak, the results screen is not delivering on the promise the ad and page made. The most common cause: the assessment output feels generic. If the AI is producing level labels without specific behavioral observations, the diagnosis moment fails and the offer lands cold. Fix output specificity before testing variants.

Soft numbers are almost never an ad problem at NPI stage. They are a product promise problem. The ad got the click. The call or the results screen did not close it.
